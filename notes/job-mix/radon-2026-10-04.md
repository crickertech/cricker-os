# Five job-mix boots on radon, 2026-10-04

The bench evening [`notes/job-mix.md`](../job-mix.md) asks for in "The next bench evening on
radon", read in its step 6 order and judged against its step 7 table. It is the first silicon run of
the 2026-09-19 instrument: seven job kinds, each point the median of 21 repeats.

## The run

- radon (VisionFive 2, four online harts, `rdtime` at 4 MHz), five boots, power-cycled between
  boots by calef at the bench.
- Image from `04a8f9e6c` on `main`, built once with `script/board-image --job-mix --tftp` and
  netbooted from patagonia. The kernel measured the archive before the sweep, so each boot was
  sealed.
- Every boot printed `one of 7 kinds` and `median of 21 repeats`, six points, and `job-mix: done`.
  No `FAILED` line appeared, so 32 tasks fit in radon's memory.
- Transcripts: `bench/radon-2026-10-04/jobmix-boot1.log` to `boot5.log`, cleaned with the step 5
  `tr` filter. Each raw capture held 14 bytes outside printable ASCII, all in U-Boot's output
  before the kernel ran.

A correction to the brief this lane was given. It said boot 1 had no `tasks=1` line. All five raw
captures have one, found with plain `grep -a` and with `LC_ALL=C`, and the five raw files are
byte-identical to the backup copy. So boot 1 is complete and is counted here.

## 1. Is every point a number?

`jpm_median` per boot, and the spread between boots as (max - min) / min, the definition milestone
168's 2026-09-16 table used.

| tasks | boot 1 | boot 2 | boot 3 | boot 4 | boot 5 | median | spread | over 1 task |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 323,972 | 324,033 | 323,951 | 323,995 | 323,972 | 323,972 | 0.03% | 1.00x |
| 2 | 567,826 | 568,352 | 569,300 | 568,578 | 569,036 | 568,578 | 0.26% | 1.76x |
| 4 | 845,791 | 844,124 | 853,611 | 848,794 | 851,205 | 848,794 | 1.12% | 2.62x |
| 8 | 876,087 | 880,068 | 876,637 | 889,101 | 874,497 | 876,637 | 1.67% | 2.71x |
| 16 | 895,000 | 891,421 | 893,887 | 888,042 | 897,651 | 893,887 | 1.08% | 2.76x |
| 32 | 899,399 | 900,576 | 897,375 | 901,122 | 899,095 | 899,399 | 0.42% | 2.78x |

Yes, at every point. The bar was under 10%. The widest point is `tasks=8` at 1.67%, and `tasks=4`
is 1.12% against 29.4% on 2026-09-16 and a resampling prediction of about 4%. Boot 1 sits closest
to the five-boot median at every point, so it is the representative boot for step 8.

The 2026-09-16 page predicted, before any new boot, that the median would read about 1.73x at two
tasks where the best of three had read 1.96x. It read 1.76x. That is the statistic being honest
about the old figure, as predicted, not the kernel getting slower.

## 2. Outlier boots

`ticks_max` over `ticks_median`, per boot, in percent.

| tasks | boot 1 | boot 2 | boot 3 | boot 4 | boot 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.8 | 13.2 | 3.8 | 4.4 | 1.2 |
| 2 | 0.8 | 1.1 | 0.5 | 0.4 | 0.5 |
| 4 | 4.0 | 1.9 | 3.9 | 5.4 | 4.0 |
| 8 | 4.5 | 4.5 | 3.2 | 4.8 | 2.5 |
| 16 | 2.3 | 1.1 | 3.0 | 2.2 | 2.5 |
| 32 | 1.3 | 0.6 | 1.3 | 1.1 | 1.3 |

No boot is an outlier. The one large figure, boot 2's 13.2% at one task, is a single repeat: repeat
0 read 107,300 ticks against about 94,800 for the other twenty. Every boot's repeat 0 at one task is
its slowest (95,967 to 107,300), which is a cold first pass rather than placement. The median
discards it, which is what the median was for.

The census moved and the numbers did not. Boots 1 to 3 placed all 34 threads identically at spawn.
Boot 4 had 6/9/9/10 threads a core against 7/8/9/10. Boot 5 differed most: core 2 held three of the
first five tasks and the second server shared core 4 with task 0. Boot 5 is within 0.6% of the
median at every point. So the placement effect `notes/soak.md` measured at up to fifteenfold did not
appear here. One caution: the census is placement at spawn, and the kernel steals work between
cores, so it is not where a task ran during a subrun. In every boot the first four tasks were
spawned onto three cores.

## 3. The shape

From the medians: 1.76x at two tasks, 2.62x at four, then 2.71x, 2.76x and 2.78x at 8, 16 and 32.
It stops rising steeply at four, the core count, and keeps creeping upward through 32. Nothing past
the knee declines, on any boot. The 2026-09-16 shape (up to about four, then flat through 32) held
under the new statistic and the heavier mix.

Two readings of the number at four, so neither is taken for the other. 2.62x on four harts is 65%
of linear. Part of that is the instrument's own design: two echo servers serve every task's
`round_trip` job, so four tasks contend 2:1 for the first time. Part may be placement, since four
tasks were spawned onto three cores.

## 4. Which kinds pay

`per_job` in 4 MHz ticks, the median of the five boots, and its ratio at 32 tasks over 1. The ratio
was within 0.1 of these on every boot except `yield` (54.6x to 58.7x).

| kind | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 32 / 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| compute | 2,409 | 2,454 | 2,558 | 2,665 | 2,771 | 2,862 | 1.19x |
| touch | 782 | 794 | 823 | 862 | 885 | 914 | 1.17x |
| null_syscall | 108 | 137 | 202 | 211 | 220 | 236 | 2.18x |
| yield | 85 | 128 | 225 | 1,248 | 3,097 | 4,726 | 55.6x |
| round_trip | 498 | 677 | 1,390 | 3,980 | 10,563 | 25,990 | 52.2x |
| map | 360 | 447 | 625 | 672 | 701 | 756 | 2.10x |
| spawn | 1,172 | 1,384 | 1,516 | 2,572 | 3,251 | 3,849 | 3.28x |

`map` and `spawn` with `region_ticks` (the region lock's `SPLIT` and `DESTROY`) taken out, per job:

| kind | part | 1 | 4 | 32 | 32 / 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| map | region calls | 107 | 150 | 167 | 1.56x |
| map | the rest | 253 | 475 | 589 | 2.33x |
| spawn | region calls | 162 | 216 | 243 | 1.50x |
| spawn | the rest | 1,010 | 1,300 | 3,606 | 3.57x |

What each row means, and where it is not a kernel cost.

- `per_job` is self-timed wall clock, so a job charges every tick its task spent off the CPU
  (crates/job_mix's `BUGS`). `compute` and `touch` never enter the kernel and grew 1.17x to 1.19x.
  That is the preemption baseline.
- `yield` and `round_trip` give the CPU away by definition. Their wall time is mostly other threads
  running: at 32 tasks a core holds 6 to 10 threads, and 16 tasks share each echo server. Their 52x
  to 56x is queueing, not a slower path. At 32 tasks `round_trip` is 80% of all self-timed task
  time (boot 1), so the top of the curve is largely a measure of how fast two shared servers are
  served.
- `null_syscall` is the one row that reads as kernel cost under load. It never blocks and its job
  is short, so preemption should charge it in proportion to its length, as it does `compute`. Yet
  it nearly doubles from one task to four (108 to 202 ticks, about 0.42 us to 0.79 us a trap) while
  `compute` grows 6%. Past four it grows 17% to 32 tasks, close to `compute`'s 12%. So the trap
  costs more when more cores are busy, and does not keep costing more as threads multiply past the
  core count. Its cause is not measured; see "What this leaves open".
- The region lock grew about 1.5x and is not the main cost in either job. The rest of `map` grew
  2.33x, most of it by four tasks, which is the `null_syscall` pattern. The rest of `spawn` grew
  3.57x, most of it past four tasks, which fits a parent waiting for a new child to be scheduled.

## Against step 7

The medians match the first row of step 7 on each of its three conditions. Rises to
the core count: 2.62x at four, the steep part ending there. Then flat or rising through 32: 2.71x,
2.76x, 2.78x. Every point within 10% across boots: the widest is 1.67%. The row's reading is this:

> no architectural per-crossing cost visible at this scale on this silicon; the risk's decisive
> experiment ran and the defence held, within the caveats in the Warton section above

The caveats that reading carries, stated here so a reader does not have to collect them:

- One machine, four harts, a stated mix with no disk job, and a ceiling of 32 tasks. A knee above
  32 would not be seen.
- It is one arm. There is no event kernel here, so nothing compares with Warton's 20%.
- The plateau is partly the instrument's own two shared servers. A process-kernel stack cost would
  show as those servers serving more slowly as threads multiply, which would bend the curve down,
  and it did not. But a curve held flat by a server bottleneck is a weaker witness than one held
  flat by spare cores.
- `null_syscall` shows a per-crossing cost that rises with busy cores. It saturates at the core
  count, which is not the shape the process-kernel argument predicts (cost growing with distinct
  stacks). It is still a per-crossing cost under load, unexplained, and it stays open below.

Step 7 assigns the verdict line in `design/fatal-risks/README.md` to whoever holds that file, not
to a lane. This page is the input and does not edit it.

## What this leaves open

- The cause of the trap's 1.9x from one task to four. `notes/ipc-tables-lock-inventory.md` names
  the one global lock and an acquisition counter it never built. Whether the null syscall takes
  that lock, or this is cache-line traffic on per-core state, is the question. The measurement is
  `null_syscall`'s `per_job` against busy-core count with the lock counted, and it is recorded in
  `notes/job-mix.md`'s `BUGS`.
- Where radon runs out of memory above 32 tasks, which milestone 168 (a multi-tasking workload
  benchmark) records as a limitation.
